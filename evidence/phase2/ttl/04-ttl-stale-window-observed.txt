=== PHASE 2 Extension B — TTL CACHING AND A CONTROLLED RECORD CHANGE ===
=== Observed from Mac 1 (Naman), 2026-11-22 ===

Spec asks for: a short visible TTL; resolve the name; change the record;
observe the client still getting the OLD answer until the TTL expires; then
the new one; plus a manual cache flush. All of that is below.

TTL is 30s (Phase 1 served 0, so nothing ever cached -- see evidence/dns/04).

Record changed from the standby edge back to the primary edge at ~23:27:07.

THE STALE WINDOW
  23:27:08  the authoritative record has ALREADY changed to 10.7.11.169,
            but the client still believes 10.7.21.130 and is still sending
            its HTTPS requests to the OLD edge.
  ...this persists for about 11 seconds, four consecutive samples...
  23:27:19  the cached entry expires, the client re-resolves, and traffic
            moves to the new edge by itself. Nothing was restarted.

  That gap IS the TTL. During it, the system is genuinely split: new lookups
  go to the new edge while clients holding a cached answer keep using the old
  one. This is exactly what the spec describes for Extension E as well --
  the same observation covers both extensions.

WHY dig DOES NOT SHOW THE TTL COUNTING DOWN
  The 'auth' column is produced by dig querying dnsmasq DIRECTLY, and dnsmasq
  stamps a fresh 30s TTL on every answer. A TTL only counts down inside a
  CACHE. On macOS that cache is mDNSResponder, which is what dscacheutil,
  ping, curl and the browser all go through -- which is why the 'cached'
  column, not dig, is the one that demonstrates TTL behaviour.

WHAT PRODUCTION DOES WITH THIS
  Before a planned migration you lower the TTL well in advance, so that by
  cutover time no client can hold a stale answer for longer than that window.
  Our 30s TTL is the same idea at demo scale. It is also why DNS failover is
  never instant, and why real high availability does not rely on DNS alone.

NOTE: an earlier cut-over in the opposite direction did NOT show a stale
window, because that run restarted dnsmasq while the client was mid-poll;
the brief unavailability caused the client to re-resolve immediately rather
than serve from cache. The window above is the clean observation.
